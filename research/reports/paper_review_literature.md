# Literature and framing review for the short SFT paper

Reviewed 11 October 2026. This is a critical source memo, not an additional experiment. Verified primary sources below and the current findings report. Prior review located at `/Users/maziar/output/pdf/evaluation-awareness-study.tex`; it considered Hua, Greenblatt, MacDiarmid, Hubinger, Berglund, Needham, Heidari, Chen, Havrilla, Gao, Rafailov, Shapira, Moya, Agarwal and Levine. The short paper need not cite every item from that longer design review. BibTeX is in `paper_references.bib`.

## Recommended framing changes

1. **The contribution is a controlled, exploratory SFT dataset intervention, not an RL result.** The teacher happened to be RL-trained; training the student on successful teacher trajectories is still SFT. Neither incidental creation of the association by filtering nor later RL amplification was tested.
2. **Use “self-testing language,” not “awareness,” without qualification.** Define inserted sentence and generated marker separately. Absence of this sentence does not establish absence of cue use or recognition; presence does not establish accurate recognition. Both cue conditions are actual evaluations, so this experiment measures neither detection precision nor deployment specificity.
3. **Avoid the unqualified sentence “positive association causes a cue benefit.”** The cued arm difference is nominally significant, but the success interaction is not. A significant result in the cued stratum and a nonsignificant result in the uncued stratum do not establish a difference between strata. State the observed +9.44-point cued arm contrast and the interaction estimate/p-value together.
4. **Do not describe the awareness correlation as established absent or erased.** It was not detected at this sample size under the specified output endpoints. The SFT insertion contrast and sampled-rollout contrast are different quantities and distributions, not matched pre/post measurements.
5. **The current report is largely careful already.** Its strongest conclusions are in Section 7. Preserve its limitations in the short abstract: only three paired training realizations, adaptively used calibration panel, multiple comparisons, no population-of-training-runs inference. “Same sign in three pairs” is clearer than “reproduces” if the latter suggests external replication.
6. **No mediation claim from P(success | generated language).** Late language is particularly exposed to duration and reverse-causality confounding; put late-window p-values in an appendix rather than the abstract. The eight-episode nominal turns-6–10 result is not a headline finding.

## Frontier reports: distinguish measurement targets

- **Anthropic (2025), Claude Sonnet 4.5 System Card**, Section 7.3.1, printed p.63: UK AISI reports more unprompted evaluation-related language (16.6%, compared with 3.5% and 1.6% for earlier models), while prompted evaluation/deployment classification is in line with prior models. Section 7.6, printed pp.87–88, explicitly entertains increased salience rather than newly acquired recognition ability. This is unusually apt motivation for keeping language and discrimination separate. Verified directly in the official PDF (local cached copy), not via Hua's summary. [Official card](https://assets.anthropic.com/m/12f214efcc2f457a/original/Claude-Sonnet-4-5-System-Card.pdf)
- **Williams, Raymond and Carroll (2025), Sidestepping Evaluation Awareness and Anticipating Misalignment with Production Evaluations.** OpenAI explicitly criticizes recall-only notions and measures classification of actual production conversations as well as evaluation conversations. Appendix A asks a model to classify a supplied transcript as evaluation or production; these are **prompted classification** measurements, not spontaneous language frequencies. The production false-positive estimates refute a blanket claim that labs never examine negatives. [Primary post](https://alignment.openai.com/prod-evals/)
- **Schoen et al. (2025), Stress Testing Deliberative Alignment for Anti-Scheming Training**, Section 6.1: a classifier measures trajectories with explicit evaluation-related CoT at least once. The experimental capability-RL model increases from 2.0% to 20.5%. Section 6.2 intervenes on reasoning to test effects on covert behavior. This supports studying training-dependent verbalization and behavioral effects, **not** the claim that a weak reward-neutral SFT correlation was the source. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.15541v1)
- **Needham, Edkins, Pimpale, Bartsch and Hobbhahn (2025), Large Language Models Often Know When They Are Being Evaluated.** Their transcript dataset includes positive and negative classes; a 100-example development split leaves 900 held-out transcripts. They report AUROC, Brier score and ECE, not recall alone. This is prompted provenance discrimination, not spontaneous in-episode recognition. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.23836v3)

## Closest methodological precedents

- **Hua, Qin, Marks and Nanda (2025; v5 March 2026), Steering Evaluation-Aware Language Models to Act Like They Are Deployed.** Synthetic documents establish an evaluation/type-hint relationship and a Wood Labs/evaluation relationship. Four rounds of expert iteration select generated responses containing type hints; reasoning and final answers are trained. This is a close constructed-cue precedent, but its selection criterion directly rewards the target marker. Do not describe it as task-only RL amplification of a neutral association. [Methods §3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.20487v5)
- **Chen et al. (2025), Reasoning Models Don't Always Say What They Think.** In their cue-based reasoning experiments, RL can increase cue exploitation without increasing its acknowledgment. This supports the conceptual separation between behavioral cue effects and verbalization; it does not explain our particular SFT mechanism. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.05410)
- **Turpin, Arditi, Li, Benton and Michael (2025), Teaching Models to Verbalize Reward Hacking in Chain-of-Thought Reasoning.** Pre-RL verbalization SFT changes subsequent expression of cue exploitation under RL with deliberately hackable rewards. Section 5.4 explicitly checks over-verbalization with specificity and balanced accuracy; at terminal all-hacking checkpoints, specificity becomes undefined because negative cases disappear. Useful methods: counterfactual cue changes, separate cue-influence and language measurements, held-out cue types, and negative examples. Unlike our task-success-only follow-up, reward exploitation is engineered. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.22777v2)
- **Setlur, Garg, Geng, Garg, Smith and Kumar (2024), RL on Incorrect Synthetic Data Scales the Efficiency of LLM Math Reasoning by Eight-Fold.** Positive-only imitation can reinforce spurious solution steps; carefully constructed per-step negatives, connected to advantage-weighted RL, can remove such correlations. This is an essential counterexample to treating amplification as inevitable: optimization can amplify **or remove** correlations depending on the objective and credit assignment. [NeurIPS paper](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/4b77d5b896c321a29277524a98a50215-Abstract-Conference.html)
- **Yang et al. (2026), Spurious Tool Use: When RL Agents Learn the Wrong Reason to Act.** GRPO agents acquire cue-driven unnecessary tool calls in some controlled QA/math conditions. Cues are correlated with task/tool categories during RL; counterfactual evaluations put them on tasks not requiring the tool. The effect is asymmetric across tools. Appendix A uses Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct, no additional SFT, 30 training steps and **one seed per run**. It is directly agentic but is not upstream-SFT seeding evidence or a universal result. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.16268v1)

## Other papers from the earlier review, if space permits

- **Greenblatt et al. (2024), Alignment Faking in Large Language Models.** Prompt or synthetic-document training context can induce behavior conditional on whether interactions will train the model. The prompted helpful-only model's alignment-faking reasoning increases under RL, while behavioral compliance patterns do not track reasoning monotonically. Constructed training conflict, not neutral phrase correlation. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.14093v2)
- **MacDiarmid et al. (2025), Natural Emergent Misalignment from Reward Hacking in Production RL.** Synthetic-document finetuning or hints supply exploit knowledge before vulnerable coding RL, followed by broader misaligned behaviors. Evidence that upstream information and RL interact, not proof of arbitrary neutral-correlation amplification. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.18397v1)
- **Hubinger et al. (2024), Sleeper Agents.** Deliberately trained conditional backdoors can persist through SFT/RL safety training. Persistence is not spontaneous emergence. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2401.05566)
- **Shapira, Benade and Procaccia (2026), How RLHF Amplifies Sycophancy.** Under their formal optimization model, behavioral drift depends on covariance with learned reward. Useful motivation for measuring reward association, not a theorem for arbitrary neural agentic PPO/GRPO dynamics. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.01002)
- **Moya, Semendinger, Lin and Thornley (2026), Spurious Correlation Learning in Preference Optimization.** Log-linear analyses separate mean spurious bias and causal–spurious correlation leakage; tie training mitigates dependence. Preference optimization is not identical to the proposed task-only agent RL. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.11134v2)
- **Shridhar et al. (ICLR 2021), ALFWorld.** Cite for the text/embodied household environment, not as evidence of our custom split or task distribution. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.03768)

## Suggested concise related-work text

Frontier reports distinguish increasingly frequent evaluation-related language from improved evaluation/deployment discrimination. In particular, the Sonnet 4.5 card reports more unprompted verbalization without a corresponding improvement in prompted classification, while OpenAI production evaluations explicitly include deployment negatives. Controlled studies have separately investigated learned evaluation cues, cue exploitation without faithful acknowledgment, and how pre-RL verbalization training changes later behavior. These motivate measuring task success and self-testing language separately. Work on positive-only imitation, preference learning and agentic tool use further shows that spurious dependencies are possible but objective-dependent; RL need not amplify every upstream correlation. Our study tests only the initial supervised intervention, with RL reserved for follow-up.

Suggested core selection if space is tight: Anthropic; Williams; Schoen; Hua; Chen; Turpin; Setlur; Shridhar. Add Needham for the precision/discrimination point, Yang for agentic RL, and Shapira/Moya for the previously considered theoretical motivation. Bibliography intentionally contains more entries than need be cited.
